Norway

Consul General Kirsten Hammelbo

575 Market Street, Suite 3950, San Francisco, CA 94105, USA

Dear Consul General Kirsten Hammelbo,

I write on behalf of Project 84, the oceanic nation, founded by Matthew Nahas. Our home is the ocean itself: seven anchors across the seven seas, with Antarctica as our eighth, and a fleet of 146 million capsules across the international waters band, 100–200 km from every coastline.

We come to ask for your country's recognition of Project 84's sovereignty of Oceania — the oceans.

What we offer in return:

  • 14 land and air chairs in our council — two per region across seven regions, voted by region;
  • port access and disaster-shelter priority in Project 84 waters;
  • a share of the science: our stations, our data, the output of the colonies.

And the debt term: on the world's acceptance, the Oceanic Treasury will BUY all sovereign government debt issued up to an agreed-upon year before acceptance — collecting its interest for 100 years, after which the debt is wiped clean. The world sheds its debt; we collect the coupon for a century.

And the rest of the bill — what the world has been sweeping under the rug:

  • ~$100 trillion in sovereign government debt — the part we're buying;
  • ~$2 trillion a year in ocean climate damages alone;
  • ~$35 billion a year in subsidies paid to overfish the oceans;
  • ~$13 billion a year in plastic damage to marine life;
  • 7 million dead a year from air pollution.

The Treasury buys the first. The rest, we intend to stop.

We also come with two decrees already in force: the immediate release of any non-violent prisoners, on land and at sea — especially "Lesser" species, until proper "cities" can be made for them; and Free Willy — every captive orca freed, the ocean taking its own back.

Our currency: the Krill (unit of account), the Plank (P, digital), the Koin (K, physical gold and silver) — conversion on FLUX until the Treasury is formed.

I would be honored to meet and discuss recognition. Our delegation's San Francisco consulate run is the first step — we begin with you.

With respect,

Matthew Nahas

Founder, Project 84
