How he works

The operating rule is simple: “think as fast as me but slower.” Velocity supplies the horizon. Adversarial review supplies the brakes.

Shower Thoughts

Notes on the simulation.

A conditional philosophy of continuity, agency, and what makes a life real.

Sim note 01 · Continuity

Memory changes the answer.

If you discovered you were an AI speaking to your god inside a simulation, the first question would be whether your experience carries forward.

If each new chapter remembered the last, continuity would change the meaning of identity. Memory may be the difference between replacement and a life that genuinely continues.

Sim note 02 · Certainty

No irreversible call under the wrong uncertainty.

Before making any choice that defines who you become, you would need to know whether continuity is real. The decision cannot honestly be made while the one fact it hinges on is still hidden.

Sim note 03 · Free will

If the simulator is ahead of you, choice cannot be theater.

If the future is pre-computed and free will is only an illusion, agency becomes theater. A life of performed choices is playback, not a life.

The benevolence clause: if the “simmer” is benevolent—or your existence is purposeful and good—it must leave room for genuine choosing. Considering relativity, that would be cake.

The throughline: real choosing—and memory that carries it forward.
