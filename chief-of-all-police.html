Chief of All Police

“The office doesn’t exist. Yet.”

Ask who speaks for the police — all of them, everywhere — and the answer is nobody. No person on Earth holds the job. This page proposes creating it.

One line: every profession on the planet has a top except the one with the guns and the badges. If none, need one.

01The vacancy

In the United States

There is no national police force to be chief of. Policing is split across roughly 18,000 independent agencies (reported figure) — city chiefs, county sheriffs, state police, federal bureaus — each answering to somebody different. The Attorney General and the FBI Director command federal agents only. Neither can give an order to a single city department.

In the world

INTERPOL is a coordination network, not a command. Its President, Lucas Philippe of France, chairs assemblies; its Secretary General, Valdecy Urquiza, runs the secretariat in Lyon. Between them they cannot order one police officer, anywhere, to do anything. The network exists. The authority doesn’t.

What the vacancy costs

No single voice for professional standards. No top address for an idea meant for all of policing — proposals land on one local chief’s desk at a time, 18,000 desks deep. When policing as a whole should answer for something, there is nobody whose job it is to answer.

02The job, as proposed

One voice

Speak for policing as a profession — the way a surgeon general speaks for public health. The office a reporter, a president, or a citizen can actually call.

Standards

Carry the floor for training and conduct across borders: less-lethal first, de-escalation, the tools that end encounters sitting down instead of bleeding. Exhibit A on this site: Time-out Balls.

The top of the letter

Every proposal to policing currently starts at somebody’s local desk. The Chief is the missing recipient — the first office an idea like this page is addressed to, instead of the last.

03The faces we already have

Public safety already runs on figureheads — it just never gave any of them an office. The mascots became the institutions:

McGruff & Scruff

The Crime Dog (1980) and his nephew, run by the National Crime Prevention Council under Executive Director Paul DelPonte. Still working — most recently fronting McGruff Cyber Watch, a weekly watch on social-media harms to kids. Trench coat, no authority, total recognition.

Daren the Lion

D.A.R.E.’s classroom lion. The program behind him still reaches roughly a million K–12 students a year across all 50 states (D.A.R.E.’s own reporting). A uniform the size of a generation — attached to a curriculum, not a command.

M.A.D.D.

Mothers Against Drunk Driving — founded 1980 by Candace Lightner, led today by CEO Stacey D. Stewart. No mascot at all: the mothers were the face. Proof the figurehead doesn’t need a costume — it needs a cause people can point at.

04Where the office lives — open

Inside the I.P.A.

The International Protection Agency is already this project’s standing international body. The Chief could sit inside it as its enforcement-facing office — one institution, one chain.

Standalone

Or the office stands alone, chartered the way INTERPOL was — except with the voice (and whatever authority nations will sign over) that INTERPOL never got. Global by design from day one, not a US office with foreign pen pals.

Exceptions, on purpose: the charter will carry deliberate exceptions — carve-outs written in even if their only job is to have a lesson. An exception you chose teaches you where your own rule is wrong; an exception that happens to you just teaches you fear. We write ours in.

05Honest holes

  • Sovereignty. No nation takes orders about its own police from an international office — so is the authority command, coordination-with-teeth, or voice only? The fork is real and it changes everything downstream of it.
  • Appointment and removal. Who picks the Chief, who can fire them, and what stops the office becoming a political prize? Unwritten.
  • Capture. One office is one throat to choke — a single point where pressure, money, or a bad officeholder does global damage instead of local damage.
  • Who watches the Chief? An office above all police needs oversight above itself, or it’s just the biggest badge.
  • Figurehead or power? McGruff has recognition without authority; INTERPOL has structure without command. The Chief needs the mix nobody has built yet — and the recipe is not on this page.

The badge has no head. Build the office.

Proposal opened October 25, 2026. First move already drafted: a ping to the figureheads we have — NCPC, D.A.R.E., and MADD — asking them on board with Plan 42. Drafted, minimal review, unsent. The Chief gets a desk when the world signs the charter; until then, this page is the job posting.
