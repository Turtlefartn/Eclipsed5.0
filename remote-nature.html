Project 84 · Plan 42 · A shared goal

Give the land back.

Make more remote areas where nature can take back over. Larger national parks — and more of them.

100%The target. And we’ll take whatever is closest to it.
100–200 kmWhat “remote” means: that far from any Plan 42 city — the exact line slides with how many cities there are. (62–124 miles.)
17.6%Of the world’s land is protected today. That is the starting line, not the finish. Protected Planet Report 2024.
8.3M acresThe largest US national park today, Wrangell–St. Elias. Bigger is possible. More is the point.

Concentrate people. Free the land.

Plan 42 builds dense cities instead of sprawl. Project 84 builds homes at sea instead of subdivisions. Every person housed that way is land that is never cleared, fenced, or cut up — land that can join a park, grow a park, or become a new one. The cities and the lifeboats aren’t the opposite of the wilderness. They’re how you pay for it.

Managed, not abandoned

These aren’t empty places nobody looks after. They’re managed nature — restored where it’s broken, watched, and then left to run itself.

Pod-drop ready

No hotels, no developments. A pod is dropped in, you live in it, and when it leaves, nothing stays behind but the clearing it sat on.

Vacations — or years in the stix

Come for a week, or come for years. Living out in the sticks, in a pod, is a first-class way to live — if you prefer it to a city, that choice stays yours. Nobody gets moved out to make room for nature.

Roads, but not like today

Where we’re going, we don’t need those roads. With AI driving and the new offroad “minions,” heavy cement and asphalt shouldn’t be needed. Light tracks that can fade back — not permanent cuts.

“A park you can only reach by being dropped in stays remote by construction.”

Land first — because the ocean is built out of it

This comes before Project 84 and Omega 42, for a practical reason: ocean habitats are built from materials and blocks made on land. No land production, no ocean build. For that we need the unglamorous three: laborers, math checkers, and engineers.

The factory on land — and the sky stays wild

Land first only works if the land layer actually makes things. This is the machinery that does it — and every piece of it is designed to leave the surface, and the sky, doing their jobs.

A drill that casts stone

A heated head melts its way down through rock, and the rock it displaces comes up already molten — poured straight into molds. The blocks come out as big as our airships can lift, and they become Project 84’s ocean platforms, its breakwalls, and larger ports and breakwalls along coasts everywhere. The ocean habitats are quite literally built out of the land.

Homes underneath, nature on top

The same drill hollows out domed, climate-controlled housing half a kilometre to a kilometre down. People living underground means the ground above them can be one hundred percent nature. A place to live and the 100% stop fighting over the same land.

Airships, not asphalt

Blocks and supplies move by air. Nothing gets paved, graded or cut to haul them, and the ships place loads where cranes can’t go. How high they fly doesn’t matter. What matters is they don’t come down where they shouldn’t.

Lightning, taken at the doorstep

The drill runs on captured lightning, starting where the storms are thickest — the highest free energy on Earth, free for the taking, so the work can afford to be wasteful. Strikes are captured at the lowest possible height, after the bolt has burned its full path through the sky — so the nitrogen rain still falls and the sky keeps doing its job. Even the heat coming off fresh-cast blocks gets a second use before it’s gone.

And when the building is done, the solar stays: fields in the sunniest places on Earth power whatever got built — the homes, the ports, the parks’ pods — long after the drills move on.

Honest holes

  • 100% of what, exactly? All land outside the cities is the working read — the exact boundary is still to be drawn.
  • Take-back takes time. Abandoned land doesn’t heal itself on schedule: invasives, broken rivers, missing minerals. Some places need active restoration first, then hands off.
  • Food has to live somewhere. Cities free marginal land first. Where prime farmland fits in the maths is still open.
  • A park on paper isn’t a park. Unfunded, un-managed protection is the failure mode of parks today. Managed means someone actually manages it.

Numbers on this page keep their families: the 100–200 km on this page is distance from a Plan 42 city, on land. It is not the Project 84 ocean 100–200 km capsule band. Same numbers, different jobs.
