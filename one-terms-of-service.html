A campaign for the whole internet

One Terms of Service

One terms of service, in a single cookie. One universal agreement — written so a person can actually read it — accepted once, and honored by every participating service. No more signing a different contract you've never read at every door on the internet.

The single cookie

The acceptance lives in one cookie that you hold:

one-tos = accepted · version 1 · date of acceptance

That is the whole payload. It is not a tracking cookie: it carries no identity, no history, and nothing about you. It says one thing — this person has agreed to the One Terms — and a participating site reads it instead of making you agree to its own private terms all over again.

The draft universal terms (plain language)

  1. We say what we do. A service states, in plain language, what it does with you and your data. If it isn't stated, it isn't permitted.
  2. Your data is yours. You can see it, export it, and delete it. Deletion means deletion.
  3. No dark patterns. No trick buttons, no roach-motels, no consent obtained by exhaustion.
  4. Changes are announced, not buried. A change to the terms is announced in advance, in plain language, and takes effect only for people who accept it.
  5. One agreement, everywhere. A participating service may add terms only for genuinely new risks it creates — and those additions get the same plain-language test.
  6. Leaving is a right. You can leave any service and take your data with you. No hostage data, no exit fees on your own life.

This is a campaign draft, not a finished legal instrument — see the lawyer note below.

The interpretive tests

  • The read-it test: could a normal person read the whole agreement, out loud, in one sitting — and understand it?
  • The grandmother test: would you be comfortable with your grandmother agreeing to this without a lawyer in the room?
  • The mirror test: would the company accept these same terms if the roles were reversed?
  • The front-page test: would the practice survive being described, accurately, on the front page?

A clause that fails a test doesn't ship.

How it gets real — the implementation path

  1. Publish the standard. One versioned, public document — the One Terms — maintained in the open.
  2. The cookie spec. A tiny, public technical standard for the acceptance cookie: what it may contain (almost nothing) and what it may never contain (tracking).
  3. Early adopters. Independent sites adopt first and display the mark: “One Terms honored here.”
  4. The lawyer. The campaign is hiring a lawyer to draft the One Terms of Service for the entire internet as a real legal instrument — the plain-language draft above is the brief, not the finished law.
  5. Scale. Adoption is voluntary and contagious: every site that honors the One Terms makes the next site's private 40-page contract look worse.

FAQ

Is the cookie a tracker? No. A tracking cookie follows you for someone else's benefit. This cookie speaks for you, once, and says nothing else.

Can a site still have its own rules? Yes — community rules, safety rules, and paid-tier terms are fine, as long as they pass the tests and don't contradict the One Terms.

What if I never accept? Then nothing changes for you: sites keep showing you their own terms, one at a time, forever. That's the world we already live in.

Who runs it? Nobody owns an agreement everyone holds. The document is public, versioned, and changed in the open.
