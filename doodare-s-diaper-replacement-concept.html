PUBLIC CONCEPT RECORD 04 OCT 2026 · REV 0.1

Dooda
re’s

A diaper replacement built around a bag, not a pad.

The premise: capture solid waste directly in a removable, tie-off liner shaped like a dog-waste bag—reducing the mess, bulk and whole-garment change of a conventional diaper.

01 / The idea

Catch it at the source. Remove only what is dirty.

Doodare’s separates the collection system from the garment. A body-contact outlet would hold a small disposable bag in place; after use, the bag would detach, tie off and go—closer to the logic of picking up after a dog than wrapping the entire lower body in absorbent material.

01

Capture

Solid waste enters a dedicated liner instead of spreading through an absorbent garment.

02

Detach

The small bag comes off without requiring a complete clothing or diaper change.

03

Tie off

The liner closes on itself for disposal, containing the mess at the smallest practical scale.

02 / Rough prototype

The soda-bottle-top version.

The proposed DIY thought experiment is intentionally blunt: a clean soda-bottle neck/top as the retaining collar, a dog-poop-style bag as the liner, and enough dietary fiber that the waste is firm enough for the concept to make sense. Those are the three ingredients of the sketch—not a tested device or an instruction to wear one.

  • SODA-BOTTLE TOP
  • SMALL TIE-OFF BAG
  • HIGH-FIBER PREMISE
Do not build or insert this prototype

An improvised bottle part, bag or plug can cut tissue, become lodged, block output, cause leakage, infection or worse. Fiber does not make an improvised internal device safe. Nothing on this page should be tested on a person, infant, child or animal. Any body-contact version belongs in clinician-led medical-device development with breakaway retention, biocompatible materials and supervised testing.

03 / Free CAD

Print the bag-fit bench puck.

This free model lets someone test how a small bag folds over and grips a raised collar—on a workbench only. It is a broad 90 mm puck, not the insertable component requested, and deliberately stops short of modeling a wearable medical device.

FILAMENT ANSWER

PLA is fine for this dry bench fixture. No consumer 3D-printer filament can be recommended here as “body safe” for internal contact: layer lines trap contamination, prints are porous, and colorants/additives may be undocumented. A real body-contact version would require a regulated manufacturer using certified, medically appropriate materials and ISO 10993 biocompatibility testing—not a home FDM print.

04 / Honest holes

A sharp premise with unsolved engineering.

The original version calls for a butt-plug-like retention interface. That is also the central safety failure: an internal plug is not acceptable for casual or DIY use. A serious development path would start by asking whether the same collection logic can work with a soft, external, skin-safe seal instead.

Urine is separate

The bag idea addresses solid waste. A complete diaper replacement still needs a safe urine-management layer.

Movement changes everything

Sitting, crawling, sleeping and sudden motion all stress the seal and increase pressure risk.

Skin is the boundary

Adhesion, moisture, pressure, cleaning and removal have to avoid irritation and injury.

Failure must be gentle

A safe design should detach before it pulls, tears, blocks or transfers force to the body.
