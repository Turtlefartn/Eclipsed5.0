02 / MODEL AUDIT

Three bins. No brochure language.

A number can be arithmetically correct and still be operationally useless. This separates what currently closes, what remains unproven, and what is internally out of sync.

Arithmetic holds

  • The tolerance doubling is explicit.73M becomes 146M. The reserve is visible rather than buried.
  • Population claims are separable.292M is the working-mix figure; 730M is a pure habitation ceiling. They are not the same promise.
  • The shell subtotal is reconstructable.146M × 9.3 t × $3,000/t lands near $4.1T before timber.

Still unproven

  • The 5 mm shell is a starting assumption.Fatigue, corrosion, wave loads, damage tolerance and role-specific depth need engineering.
  • The useful capsule is not costed.Propulsion, ballast, navigation, comms, autonomy, energy and role modules could materially exceed the bare structure.
  • The industrial ramp has no validated factory model.Global output share is not the same thing as fabrication capacity for millions of finished marine units.

Version drift

  • 5 × 15 and 15 × 10 coexist.The current headline uses a 75 m² footprint; the old per-unit geometry remains elsewhere and must be retired or reconciled.
  • 146M and 500M coexist.Role mixes, build ramps, output forecasts and lifecycle math still carry the older fleet count.
  • Program totals inherit stale mass.Sea, land and air totals cannot be trusted until every dependent sheet is recalculated from one frozen unit spec.
03 / DEBT TEST

The $440T is a century of coupons—not a pile of cash.

At the working assumptions, $110T of debt at a 4% coupon produces $4.4T per year and $440T nominal over 100 years. Discount the future payments and the value falls back toward the principal. Then the legal mechanics decide whether any payment flows in at all.

“Assume the debt” points the cash flow the wrong way.

If the Treasury assumes governments’ obligations, it becomes the debtor and pays the coupon. To receive interest, it must instead own the creditor claims—through purchase, assignment or a consensual exchange.

DebtorTakes the obligation. Pays interest and principal.
CreditorOwns the bonds. Receives interest, but must acquire the claims.
JubileeWiping principal after 100 years assigns a loss. The instrument must say who accepts it and why.

Bottom line: the coupon is large enough to discuss. It is not available capital until the ownership and recognition mechanics exist.

05 / ORDER OF OPERATIONS

What has to become real first.

The shortest path is not “raise trillions.” It is to remove the uncertainties that make the trillions meaningless.

Freeze one capsule specification.

Resolve 5 × 15 versus 15 × 10, define shell geometry, displacement, payload, draft, stability and the minimum equipment bay.

blocks every downstream total

Build a bill of materials.

Separate hull, structure, coatings, propulsion, power, controls, comms and role modules. Price an actual finished unit, not a surface shell.

engineering + procurement

Prove one manufacturing cell.

Cycle time, labor, defects, energy, yard footprint and launch method turn “share of global output” into a production plan.

pilot before ramp

Reconcile every dependent model.

Rebuild role mix, support population, annual ramp, maintenance, revenue and three-domain mass from the frozen 146M baseline.

one source of truth

Choose the legal debt mechanism.

Decide whether the Treasury buys creditor claims, novates obligations, or proposes a new instrument. “Assume” and “receive” cannot stay in the same sentence without this.

law before coupon

Audit Plan 42 site by site.

Existing grids, rail and water make the low case; full greenfield systems make the high case. The mix decides the number.

260 local answers

Not fantasy. Not financed. Not finished.

The capsule program is small enough in raw material flow to keep studying and large enough in systems complexity to punish every shortcut. The next win is not a prettier total. It is one unit, one factory cell and one legal instrument that survive contact with specialists.
