Main Quest · A Brief for the G20

Putting the fires out while the Titanic sinks and the champagne still flows...

The band is playing. The bar is open. And below the waterline, the ship is — technically, measurably — on fire. Twice, on two continents, we set the ground alight, worked out how to put it out, priced the job, and declined to pay. Not for lack of a method. For lack of a budget line.

Exhibit A — Centralia, Pennsylvania. Burning since 1962.

An anthracite fire under a Pennsylvania borough. The town was bought out and demolished. The fire was not. A 1983 federal study priced digging it out at upward of $600 million; the government instead relocated the town for $42 million — and once the town was empty, the "public danger" that unlocks funding no longer existed. The fire sits at Priority 2 to this day: not dangerous to the public, because the public was removed. A $60 million foam proposal was later declined as too experimental. A geologist's containment "box" plan got positive feedback, and no action.

HONESTY BOX — Pennsylvania's own bureau says this fire's total CO₂ is "very difficult to accurately measure," and no measured total exists. A circulating figure of ~1.19 Gt CO₂ is rejected here: it implies ~433 Mt of coal burned, roughly 17× all the coal that was ever under the borough.

Exhibit B — Darvaza, Turkmenistan. Flaring since the late 1980s.

A collapsed Soviet-era drilling crater in the Karakum Desert, ~70 m across — a tourist attraction with a methane problem. The fix is not research: Turkmenistan's own gas institute method is to not seal it, but drill production wells into the structure underneath, draw the reservoir down, and starve the fire. By mid-2025 that method had already cut the flames roughly threefold, satellite-confirmed. What remains missing is money and priority — the two things summits exist to move.

The Pattern

Nobody's emergency. Everybody's invoice.

1.The emergency classification was written for people, not for damage. Remove the people and the damage becomes unscoreable.

2.The fix is always compared against this year's budget. The damage is never compared against anything, because no ledger carries it.

3.So the cheapest projects on Earth — known method, known price, damage running at 10–100× the fix — lose to projects with better paperwork. The band plays on.

Everything else we let keep burning

Routine gas flaring — the largest fire on Earth, lit on purpose, daily

151 billion cubic metres of gas flared in 2024 — the highest since 2007 — emitting ~389 Mt CO₂-equivalent and wasting ~$63 billion of energy in a single year (World Bank Global Gas Flaring Tracker, 2025). Nine countries account for ~75% of it. Countries signed to Zero Routine Flaring by 2030 cut intensity ~12% since 2012; non-signatories increased ~25%. The commitment works. Most of the volume sits outside it.

Jharia, India — burning since 1916

~70 fires across India's prime coking-coal field; 37 million tons of coal consumed so far, with people still living on top of it. Estimates of coal made inaccessible conflict wildly (14 Mt to ~2 Bt across sources) and are left unmerged here.

China's coalfields — the largest problem by volume

100+ major fire areas across Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia and Ningxia; coal consumed or sterilised estimated anywhere from 10 to 200 Mt per year, as reported. Globally, underground coal fires burn roughly a billion tons of coal a year — ~1,000 GW thermal — a mid-sized nation's entire power sector, unmetered, unowned, unpriced.

The United States — 100+ more

The federal inventory lists over 100 abandoned-mine fires across nine states. Centralia is merely the one with a name. The Pittsburgh-seam fire at White Hall, West Virginia is burning right now, with underground temperatures above 600 °C.

Gas fires we turned into tourist attractions

Yanar Dag in Azerbaijan, the Chimaera flames of Yanartaş in Türkiye, Baba Gurgur in Iraq, Mrapen in Indonesia. Some are natural seeps — and treating an industrial leak and a natural seep as the same object, because both photograph well, is exactly the filing error this brief is about.

The contrast class — fires that are not absurd

Burning Mountain, Australia has smouldered for at least ~6,000 years, advancing a metre a year, ignited naturally before mining existed. The Smoking Hills of Canada and Germany's Brennender Berg (1668) are likewise geology. Nobody proposes extinguishing geology. The absurdity is that human-started, human-fixable fires get filed in the same drawer — and forgotten with it.

The ask

ASK 01

One inventory

A single public, satellite-verified global register of uncontrolled combustion — coal fires, gas craters, routine flaring — each entry carrying an annual damage figure, counted the low-end way used here.

ASK 02

A fund with a trap-door

A standing extinguishment fund with a priority override: where low-end accumulated damage exceeds the quoted fix by 10×, the project funds automatically — whether or not anyone still lives on top of it. Centralia's trap should be impossible by construction.

ASK 03

Close the flaring gap

Universal adoption and enforcement of Zero Routine Flaring by 2030, with the nine largest flaring states reporting against satellite data — not self-declaration.

Change what counts.

Two holes in the ground have burned for a combined century because each was, on paper, not worth fixing. On paper of a different kind — the kind that counts the damage — they were the best bargains in the entire climate portfolio. The G20 writes the paper. Preferably before the band stops playing.
