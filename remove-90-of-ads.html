01 / The premise

Advertising can fund the internet without occupying it. The target is simple enough to remember and large enough to matter: remove nine placements out of every ten.

The 90/10 standard

A practical line for publishers, platforms, browsers and regulators—not a wish for advertising to vanish.

01

Cut the surface

Reduce the number and area of paid placements by 90% against a clearly declared baseline. Fewer slots. Higher standards.

02

End surveillance pricing

The remaining ads may use the page’s context—not a dossier assembled from a person’s behavior across the web.

03

Make ads interactive

The useful ten percent should earn attention with viewer-chosen polls, sponsor overlays and formats people actively open or join—not interruptions they have to sit through.

04

Keep live sport live

TV—especially football and live sport—should barely leave the action for commercials. Fit optional ads around natural stoppages instead of adding more breaks.

05

Publish the count

Show ad density in plain language and let independent audits test the claim. “90% fewer” must be measurable.

Live stays live.

Football, live TV and live sport should barely break for commercials. Keep the event on screen and make the ads optional, interactive and worth engaging with.

Fewer commercial breaks. More viewer choice. The game comes first.

What goes

  • Pop-ups and takeover screens
  • Ads disguised as navigation
  • Cross-site behavioral tracking
  • Infinite repeat impressions
  • Autoplay sound and video
  • Commercial breaks that pull viewers out of live play

What can stay

  • Clearly labeled placements
  • Interactive ads viewers choose to open
  • Low-profile sponsor overlays during live TV
  • Formats built around natural stoppages
  • Contextual, not behavioral, targeting
  • One useful ad instead of ten

This is not a fragile market.

Digital advertising represented 67.6% of worldwide ad spending in Dentsu’s December 2025 forecast. The campaign asks a mature industry to compete on usefulness—not volume.

Source: Dentsu, Global Ad Spend Forecasts, Dec. 2025. “90% fewer” refers to ad placements and occupied surface, not a claim that revenue will fall by the same amount.

The campaign pledge

I support an internet with 90% fewer ads: no surveillance as the price of entry, interactive ads instead of forced interruptions, barely any commercial breaks in live sport, and a clear public count of what remains.
