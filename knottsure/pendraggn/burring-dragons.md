The planet that never truly sleeps. Its surface is divided between vast, storm-wracked oceans and enormous arcology cities built upward rather than outward. Beneath the clouds, geothermal vents and deep mantle reactors provide nearly all usable energy; above them, kilometer-high towers pierce the atmosphere, their mirrored surfaces reflecting the perpetual neon of the cities below. At the summit of Cyr Earth stands the Hall of Concord, where two women face one another across an empty throne.

The woman to the left represents Ancient House Auric, an old industrial dynasty whose black-and-gold aesthetic reflects its control over Earth's physical infrastructure. Its fleets operate the orbital solar collectors, its companies maintain the planetary power grid, and its private security forces control the elevators connecting the city's districts. Its current leader, Lady Seraphine Vale, believes stability is humanity's highest achievement. Her doctrine is simple: those capable of maintaining civilization have earned the authority to govern it. To Auric, freedom without infrastructure is merely another word for collapse.

Opposite her sits Nyx Aradan, leader of Ancient House Nocturne. Her faction grew from Earth's lower cities, where independent technologists, information brokers, artists, and black-market engineers built an economy outside Auric's control. Nocturne's purple-black aesthetic is deliberate: darkness, privacy, and obscured identities are considered forms of freedom. Their greatest weapon is not an army but information. Nocturne controls communication networks, encrypted financial systems, artificial-intelligence collectives, and the identities of millions of citizens. Aradan's philosophy is equally direct: no institution should possess enough information about humanity to rule it.

Between them stands the Throne of Meridian.

It has been empty for thirty-one years.

The Sovereign possessed authority over both Houses and, theoretically, existed above their rival interests. But the last Sovereign disappeared during the Silent Ascension, when every orbital communication array went dark for exactly eleven minutes. No body was found. No successor was recognized. The throne has remained vacant ever since.

Tonight, however, the vacancy has become impossible to ignore.

A planetary energy audit has revealed that Earth's geothermal reserves are failing far faster than either House predicted. There may be less than six months before the central grid becomes incapable of supporting the upper arcologies. Auric wants emergency control of every major reactor. Nocturne has discovered evidence that the crisis was concealed from the lower districts for years.

The women have come to the Hall to decide who receives the remaining power.

Neither intends to surrender.

But the throne has begun to glow.

Its ancient systems have detected the planetary emergency and activated for the first time since the Silent Ascension. Hidden beneath the seat is a dormant Meridian intelligence, waiting for the constitutional condition required to awaken: both Houses must willingly recognize a new Sovereign.

Outside, millions of citizens watch the Hall through hacked broadcasts.

Below them, elevators stop. Hospitals switch to generators. Water rationing begins.

And between two women who have spent their lives preparing to defeat one another, the empty throne waits—not for conquest, but for agreement.
