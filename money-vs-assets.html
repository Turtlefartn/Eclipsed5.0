A two-part thought experiment

Money is a rule.
Assets are a thing plus a rule.

Change the money and the hoard loses its language. Change the ownership rules and the assets may lose their owner. Those are two different moves.

What happens when the old money is retired—and nothing carries over?
0
They hold all of the old agreement.
01 / The split

Same balance sheet.
Different species.

Money and assets can both be counted in dollars. That does not make them the same thing.

Money

A transferable claim

A bill, coin, or bank balance is valuable because other people accept it in exchange. Its substance is the network of agreement around it.

When the accepted unit changes, the old units can stop functioning as claims. The number remains. Its purchasing power does not have to.

TOKEN + ACCEPTANCE = MONEY
Remove acceptance, and the token becomes a record of a game that ended.
Assets

A thing with an owner

Land still occupies space. A factory still makes things. A ship still moves cargo. Their usefulness does not vanish because the measuring unit changes.

The price can be translated into the new money. The underlying capacity remains.

PHYSICAL CAPACITY + OWNERSHIP = ASSET
Change the unit, and the thing is still there.

Watch what moves.

This demonstration ends acceptance without conversion. It does not change the property rules.

03 / The mechanism

The vanishing happens in the exchange.

01

The old money is accepted.

Every unit carries a claim on whatever the network will trade for it.

02

The network changes units.

The shared rule moves. Conversion terms decide whether old claims cross over—or stop.

03

The assets keep doing.

Useful capacity survives the change and is counted again in the new unit.

04 / The deeper cut

Assets are not pure physics.

The field is real. The deed is a rule. The factory is real. The legal claim saying “this is yours” is a rule.

So changing currency does not erase an asset. But changing the property system can change who controls it.

Currency rules determine which claims count as money.
Property rules determine whose claims count as ownership.

The clean version
Retire old money without conversion and the money-hoarder holds the old game. Change only the money and the asset-holder still holds the thing.

If balances convert, holders retain whatever claim the transition grants them. The actual power sits in those terms: who writes them, what crosses over, and which claims the new system agrees to honor.
