Side Quest SQ-13 · Plants

Promote Mustards.

Filed 25 October 2026 with the title as the whole brief — so this page is honest about it: the title is set, the reason is not yet. What follows is the research case for why mustards might deserve promoting, kept separate from what the founder actually intends, which is his to say.

One plant family, five jobs.

Candidate legs only — research, not intent. If they stack on the same planting, the stack is the case: the strongest leg justifies it, the rest ride free.

LEG 01

Food

Seed for condiment, leaves as greens, seed for oil — one family, three foods, grown worldwide for millennia. Promotion here is not inventing a food, it is pushing an under-priced one harder.

LEG 02

It fumigates its own soil

Mustards carry glucosinolates. Chopped into the soil, they break down into isothiocyanates that suppress fungal pathogens, nematodes and germinating weed seeds — a pesticide the plant makes itself.

LEG 03

Fast soil work

Significant biomass in 30–45 days, a deep taproot that breaks compaction, and roots that capture residual nitrogen before it leaches. Cool-season, short-season, early out of the ground.

LEG 04

It pulls poison out of ground

Indian mustard (Brassica juncea) accumulates lead, nickel, cadmium, mercury and selenium in roots and shoots — a working phytoremediator for contaminated soil and water, if the contaminated harvest has somewhere to go.

LEG 05

A crop for hard country

More drought-tolerant than other oilseeds, mature in roughly 80–95 days depending on type, germinating in soil as cold as about 4.4°C. The short, cold, dry windows other crops miss are its home turf.

LEG 06

Fuel, maybe

Ethiopian mustard (Brassica carinata) is being developed as an aviation biofuel feedstock — a different mustard doing a different job, which is either the point of the plural, or a warning about it.

Status

Title set. Reason open.

Not set, and not invented here: which mustards, why these, what “promote” does in practice — plant them, eat them, fund them, give the seed away — the first geography, or any target. Those are the founder’s calls, and this page will be rewritten around his answer when it comes.

The honest holes

Kept on the page, on purpose.

It escapes

Left to seed, mustard is a tenacious weed. Promoting a self-seeding plant next to a programme that wants nature left alone is a tension, not a detail.

It shares diseases

Brassicas host clubroot and share pests with canola, kale, cabbage and broccoli. Scale without rotation discipline builds disease.

Fumigation is not surgical

Isothiocyanates are biocides. Beneficial soil life is in the blast radius too.

Hardy has limits — and dirty harvests

Waterlogged soil stunts it and dry sand fails it. And a mustard that pulled lead out of soil is lead with leaves: remediation needs a disposal chain, not a harvest party.
