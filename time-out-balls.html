Side Project · Less-Lethal Pursuit Concept · Idea Stage

Time-out Balls

“Everybody sit down.”

A traction-denial kit for police foot pursuits. A slippery, food-grade gel delivered ahead of a fleeing person — never primarily at them — so the run ends in a sit-down instead of a tackle, a vehicle intervention, or a weapon.

One line: a dispersal agent awaiting delivery — throw it where the run is going, deny the ground its grip, and let physics do the arresting. Working technical name, for the paperwork: traction-denial dispersal system.

01Three delivery forms, three different jobs

Hand-thrown balls

Burst-on-impact shells, one medium patch per ball. Best at a chokepoint, thrown by a stationary officer: a doorway, a stairwell, an alley mouth, a car door.

GROUND DENIAL: #2

Slime guns

Nickelodeon-style, a continuous low-pressure stream that can be corrected mid-arc. Best for laying a line across an exit or a sidewalk before the run arrives.

GROUND DENIAL: #1

Paintball / pepper-ball launcher

Already a fielded police platform — training, carriage and use-of-force policy largely exist. Tiny volume per round, so worst for ground coverage, best for coating shoe soles and lower legs at range. Legally a less-lethal projectile, with matching reporting and eye-risk burdens.

SOLE DENIAL AT RANGE: #1

02Three payload classes — deliberately never interchangeable

Tasty — the main payload

Water-based polymer gel, food-grade and flavoured, in the lineage of the U.S. Mobility Denial System anti-traction gel. Bright washable dye so officers can see their own patch. No sugar (dries tacky, kills the slick), no major allergens, sealed single-use rounds because food gels spoil.

Food-grade is a liability design choice, not a gimmick: a wrong-person hit — an “our bad” — becomes an apology, a towel, and a dry-cleaning voucher shipped in the kit. A story told laughing, not a lawsuit.

Expanding foam — ground and barrier only

Soft, non-curing, culinary-style foam. Expansion is a volume multiplier — the only payload where small rounds can build a real ground obstacle in a doorway. Never on a person. Hardening construction foams and historical sticky-foam weapons are excluded: smothering risk, solvent cleanup, skin injury, and responder entanglement killed those programs.

Sulfur stink — the marking class

A stink payload for marking and flushing: a hit person cannot hide or blend in, and sheds marked clothing that becomes evidence. Distinct colour, shape and pouch; deliberate load for a confirmed target only.

Not food-grade, and never a surprise substitute for the tasty payload — the whole wrong-person defence depends on that separation. Known cost: rotten-egg odour reads to the public as a gas leak, so outdoor use and advance notice to dispatch are required.

03Where it works, where it doesn’t

StrongWeak or useless
Smooth concrete and tileGrass, dirt and gravel — the gel is absorbed
Doors, stairs and alley mouths — chokepoints the run must crossRough asphalt — consumes volume; roughly 3–5 balls of ~200 ml per usable patch (Fermi estimate, an input not a spec)
Deployed ahead of the run, by a stationary officerRain, and any patch shorter than a running stride

04Honest holes

  1. Friendly fire is built in. The pursuing officer reaches the same slick patch at full speed, seconds later. Doctrine must be throw-and-flank, never throw-and-follow.
  2. The fall is the effect. A sprinter losing traction falls uncontrolled on concrete; head-injury risk may undercut the less-lethal claim and is the concept’s largest legal exposure.
  3. Persistence liability. A patch that stays slick endangers bystanders afterwards. The kit needs a paired neutralizer dropped after an arrest, and a gel that dries to a sweepable residue.
  4. The grip paradox. Coat hands and a person cannot climb; coat them fully and officers cannot hold them for cuffing. Ground-first dosing avoids it; direct hits create it.
  5. Naming risk in court. “Time-out Balls” honestly describes a pause, not a punishment — but the name on use-of-force paperwork stays the boring technical one. A playful name quoted after an injury helps the other side.

05The cheapest test that answers it

Toy slime blaster, throwable shells, a paintball marker with washable fill — diluted food-grade gel plus food colouring, timed sprints on concrete vs. asphalt vs. grass. Two questions only: (a) does shoe-sole coating alone stop a run on far less volume than ground coating, and (b) how long until an officer in the same footwear can safely cross the patch?

Not a general pursuit tool. A situational kit — marginal on open ground, potentially decisive at a doorway.
