HEAR-TS™The sea’s short-haul work boats

A proposed fleet of hydrogen trimarans built to move people, freight, and printable repairs between Project 84’s ocean settlements and shore.

01 / Mission

Capillaries, not container ships.

Role boundary: HEAR-TS are coastal and inter-site craft. They are not intended as deep-ocean vessels.

The seven Project 84 settlements need a vessel class for the distance between: too large for a tender, too frequent for heavy ocean freight, too essential to leave to improvised charter.

HEAR-TS fill that gap. They shuttle between settlements, run to the nearest land port, and carry enough capacity to work as either a passenger vessel or a freight platform.

03 / Onboard fabrication
A HEAR-TS craft that prints its own spares does not wait on shore logistics.

Every maintenance or work variant carries an onboard 3D printer. The design target is to maximize the printable fraction of the vessel—measured both by replaceable part count and by mass.

That doctrine turns each craft into a moving repair node for itself and the route infrastructure it serves.

05 / Unsolved

The holes stay visible.

This is a design record, not a claim that engineering is complete.

Hydrogen bunkering

Co-located production, storage, safety systems, and refueling capacity at each settlement remain to be designed.

Route scheduling

Twenty-one inter-settlement pairs plus seven shore runs create a real network-planning problem, not just a multiplication exercise.

Heavy-weather envelope

The safe operating limits for a 50-metre trimaran across open-ocean legs need specialist naval architecture and sea-state analysis.

Shore-side capacity

Seven land ports must absorb settlement-scale passenger and freight traffic without becoming the network bottleneck.
